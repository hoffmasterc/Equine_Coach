# FormSeat — Investor Overview

**AI-powered position coaching for eventing riders, grounded in real judging standards.**

---

## The Problem

Amateur eventing and dressage riders can't see their own position while riding. Getting expert feedback means booking a private lesson — averaging **$60–120/hour** in the US, more for credentialed instructors — and many riders train solo, between lessons, or at barns without a resident coach. The feedback loop that actually improves position (a knowledgeable eye, in the moment) is expensive, scheduled, and often unavailable exactly when a rider needs it: right after a ride, on their own time.

## The Solution

FormSeat lets a rider upload a single side-on photo and get an instant, standards-referenced breakdown of their position — scored separately for the dressage phase and the show-jumping phase, across six alignment points judges actually look at (vertical alignment, hands, leg, seat, head, back). Every finding cites a real, compiled reference of published USEA/FEI position standards, and the app tells the rider when a photo doesn't give it enough information to be confident — rather than always producing a polished-looking score regardless of input quality.

## Why Now

- AI vision models have crossed the threshold where structured, specific, non-generic photo feedback is possible cheaply and instantly.
- A handful of competitors (Equus AI, Ridesum, Rider's Position, Equestrian AiK) have already validated consumer willingness to try AI-based riding feedback — the category is proven, not speculative.
- None of the current entrants lead with **cited, sourced standards** and **honest confidence signaling**. That's the trust gap FormSeat is built to close, in a category where credibility with a skeptical, safety-conscious audience is the actual competitive currency.

## Market

- ~7.2M active amateur riders in the US; ~4.6M Americans involved in the equestrian industry overall (USEF/USEA).
- This is a **focused niche SaaS opportunity**, not a mass-market consumer play — realistic near-term traction looks like thousands of engaged subscribers in the competitive eventing/dressage community, acquired through barns, USEA regional groups, and riding creators/influencers, not broad paid consumer acquisition.
- Precedent: multiple direct competitors have already launched apps in this exact space, which de-risks demand but means execution and trust — not category creation — is the game.

## Business Model

Freemium subscription: limited free analyses to build trust and demonstrate value, paid monthly plans for unlimited analyses, full correction-tip detail, and (roadmap) progress tracking over time. Illustrative pricing validated in the existing prototype design: ~$9–19/month tiers.

## Competitive Moat

1. **Sourced, citable standards reference** — every finding traces to a real standards entry, not just model-generated prose. Competitors currently lead with scores/badges, not sourcing.
2. **Confidence-aware feedback** — says when a photo doesn't support a claim, instead of always sounding certain. Builds trust with a discipline-sport audience that is naturally skeptical of black-box AI advice.
3. **Phase-specific rubric depth** (dressage vs. jumping standards) rather than one generic "position score."
4. **Roadmap to computer-vision pose estimation** (Phase 4) — a path to genuinely precise, defensible measurement claims that most current entrants haven't executed on yet.
